Medicare and ACA News: 6 Changes From the First Half of October 2026

By Ally Valdez October 15, 2026 7 min read

The first two weeks of October brought more health insurance news than most full months. Some of it puts money in your pocket right now, like a one-time $90 Medicare payment and $500 ACA refund checks. Some of it changes what you should look for when you compare plans for 2027, like new Medicare star ratings and insurers pulling out of the ACA Marketplace.

Here is everything that changed, in plain English, and what (if anything) you need to do about it.

1. Medicare Open Enrollment Is Open, and Over 3 Million People Need a New Plan

Medicare's Fall Open Enrollment started today, October 15, and runs through December 7, 2026. Any change you make takes effect January 1, 2027.

This is the third straight year of big shake-ups in Medicare Advantage. More than 3 million people are in Advantage plans that were terminated or are leaving their county for 2027 and have to pick something new. If your plan is ending, you should have received a non-renewal letter by early October. You get extra time to choose and a guaranteed right to buy certain Medigap plans, but only for a limited window.

We covered the plan cuts, county exits, and your deadlines in detail in Is Your Medicare Advantage Plan Changing for 2027?

2. New 2027 Star Ratings: Fewer Top-Rated Plans

On October 8, CMS released the 2027 Medicare Advantage and Part D star ratings. CMS added new measures and raised many scoring thresholds this year, and it showed:

  • About 37% of Advantage plans with drug coverage earned 4 stars or higher, down from 44% in 2026.
  • The average rating slipped to 3.99 stars, from 4.01.
  • Only 15 contracts earned 5 stars, down from 22.
  • Humana was the big winner, with its largest contract moving back above 4 stars. UnitedHealthcare, Aetna (CVS), Elevance, Centene, and HCSC all have fewer members in 4+ star plans. Alignment Health's largest contract fell below 4 stars.

Why star ratings matter to you

Higher-rated plans often get more funding to put toward extra benefits. A 5-star plan also gives you a special enrollment period: you can switch into a 5-star plan in your area once between December 8 and November 30 of the next year. With fewer 5-star plans for 2027, that option is available in fewer places. Star ratings show up next to every plan in the Medicare Plan Finder, but they are only one factor. Your doctors, drugs, and total yearly cost still come first.

3. A One-Time $90 Medicare Part B Payment

A one-time $90 payment toward the Part B premium is going out this month to more than 20 million people with Medicare, at a total cost of about $2 billion. Here is who gets it and who does not:

  • Eligible: People enrolled in Original Medicare Part B who pay their own Part B premium.
  • Not eligible: The roughly 35 million people in Medicare Advantage, people whose Part B premium is paid by Medicaid or a state program, and (according to published eligibility summaries) people who pay the higher-income IRMAA surcharge.
  • Timing: Most eligible people were set to receive a direct deposit around October 8. Others will get a paper check from the U.S. Treasury later in October, mailed to the address Medicare has on file.
  • No application needed. The payment is automatic.

Watch for scams

Because the payment is automatic, no one from Medicare will call, text, or email asking for your bank account or Medicare number to "release" your $90. If someone does, hang up. Questions about eligibility can go to 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).

4. Social Security Gets a 3.6% Raise for 2027

On October 14, the Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027, the largest in four years. That works out to roughly $75 more per month for the average retiree, starting with January 2027 payments.

2027 Social Security cost-of-living adjustment
About $75 more per month on average. Affects nearly 72 million Social Security and 7.3 million SSI recipients. Source: Social Security Administration via CNBC, AP

One thing to keep in mind: for most people, the Part B premium is taken out of your Social Security check. The official 2027 Part B premium has not been announced yet. The 2026 Medicare Trustees Report projected about $209.50 a month, up from $202.90, which would eat a small part of that raise. CMS usually announces the final number in late October or early November, and we will update you when it does.

5. $500 ACA Refund Checks Are in the Mail

Starting September 30, the federal government began mailing one-time $500 refund checks to about 950,000 to 1 million people who buy coverage through HealthCare.gov. The administration says these enrollees were overcharged through exchange fees that were passed along in higher premiums.

  • Who gets it: HealthCare.gov enrollees in the 30 states that use the federal exchange who did not receive a premium tax credit. Most are people earning above 400% of the federal poverty level, which is a lot of self-employed people, business owners, and early retirees.
  • Households with more than one affected person may get more than one payment, by check or direct deposit.
  • It is a one-time payment. It does not lower your 2027 premiums or change your subsidy eligibility.

If you think you should have received a check and have not, keep an eye on your mail through October. State-run exchanges (like California, New York, and others) are not part of this program.

6. Seven Insurers Are Leaving the ACA Marketplace for 2027

A Commonwealth Fund analysis published October 8 confirmed that seven insurers will stop selling Marketplace plans for 2027: Baylor Scott & White Health Plan, CareSource, Cigna, Medica, Mending (formerly Taro Health), PacificSource, and Providence Health Plan. Their exits will force enrollees in 20 states to pick a new plan.

The enhanced premium subsidies that expired at the end of 2025 have still not been renewed. That means the subsidy cliff is back: a single person earning more than $63,840, or a family of four earning $132,000 or more, gets no help with premiums. The Commonwealth Fund estimates a 40-year-old just over that line who paid $445 a month in 2025 could owe about $719 a month in 2027.

ACA Open Enrollment on HealthCare.gov runs November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Choose a plan by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. A federal judge struck down a rule that would have shortened this window, and CMS says it will follow the longer schedule while the government appeals. State-run exchanges may have different dates. For income limits and subsidy math, see our 2027 ACA Marketplace Guide and the ACA resources page.

Do not let your plan auto-renew without looking

If your insurer is leaving, do not assume you will be moved into a similar plan. The replacement may not include your doctors or cover your prescriptions. And even if your plan is staying, an automatic renewal at a 15% or higher increase can cost you hundreds of dollars a month. Compare before December 15.

What to Do This Week

  • On Original Medicare? Check your bank account or mail for the $90 Part B payment. Ignore anyone who asks for your information to send it.
  • In a Medicare Advantage plan? Find your Annual Notice of Change or non-renewal letter. If your plan is ending, decide before December 7 whether to pick a new Advantage plan or move to Original Medicare with a supplement.
  • Comparing Medicare plans? Look at the new star ratings, but check your doctors, drugs, and out-of-pocket maximum first.
  • Buying your own coverage on HealthCare.gov without a subsidy? Watch for a $500 refund check, and plan to shop when Open Enrollment opens November 1.
  • With Cigna, Medica, CareSource, PacificSource, Providence, Baylor Scott & White, or Mending? Your Marketplace plan is going away. Plan to pick a new one by December 15 so there is no gap on January 1.

The Bottom Line

October 2026 brought a little extra money for some people and a lot of change for almost everyone shopping for 2027 coverage. The payments are automatic. The plan decisions are not. Whether you are on Medicare or buying your own health insurance, the next eight weeks are the time to review your options instead of letting last year's plan roll over.
